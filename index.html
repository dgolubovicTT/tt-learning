Crossing the Ridge

True/false on which performance levers actually move a kernel between the memory-bound and compute-bound regimes — and which only move you closer to a ceiling you were already under.

The idea both pages turn on

There are two ways to cross the memory/compute boundary. Move yourself right by raising arithmetic intensity — reuse, output blocking, lower-precision formats, fusion. Or move the ridge left by raising achieved bandwidth — sharding, multicast, L1-resident operands — since ridge = peak ÷ bandwidth.

Everything else — core count, batching, circular-buffer depth, problem size — moves you vertically toward a roof without ever changing which roof it is.
